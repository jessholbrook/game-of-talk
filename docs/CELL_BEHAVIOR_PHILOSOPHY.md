# Local Grammars

Vox Automata treats each talk feel as a different local grammar, not a different
clock speed. The same discrete cells and neighbor relationships remain visible
in every mode, but each grammar introduces matter through a distinct spatial
gesture: strata, halos, wandering clouds, waves, impacts, migrations, forks,
or branching colonies. The system should reveal these identities through
emergence, never through a literal icon or a canned animation.

Speech supplies pressure rather than coordinates. Level controls the amount of
matter, brightness bends orientation, texture changes concentration, and flux
decides when a structure fractures or branches. Seeded randomness keeps the
work alive while making every mutation reproducible. The meticulously crafted
algorithm must balance recognition and surprise so a feel becomes legible
within seconds but never repeats as a loop.

Neighbor rules carry the identity forward after each injection. Some grammars
permit thin structures to endure; others favor broad births, sudden clearing,
or dense survival. This gives each feel a temporal consequence as well as a
signature entrance. The master-level implementation should make the evolving
field look painstakingly tuned, with controlled population bands preventing
both empty collapse and undifferentiated noise.

Silence is part of every grammar. Glacial slabs erode slowly, meditative halos
leave sparse memory, tidal bands recede, staccato impacts clear sharply,
restless colonies migrate, electric forks burn out, and overgrown branches
remain inhabited. Beauty lives in those transitions. The final system should
feel like the product of deep computational expertise, refined until the eight
behaviors are unmistakably related yet visibly distinct.
